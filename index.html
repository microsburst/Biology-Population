Mark–recapture: estimating a population you can't count

Bio-blobs hide in long grass, so nobody can count them one by one. Catch a sample, tag it, let it go, then catch a second sample. The share of tagged blobs in the second catch tells you how big the whole population is.

  1. 1First capture (s)
  2. 2Tag them
  3. 3Release and mix
  4. 4Second capture (n)
  5. 5Count tagged (x)

Repeated trials and random sampling error

Repeats the whole method instantly with the current settings, assuming tagged blobs are fully mixed. Each bar counts how many trials gave that estimate. A wide spread means more random sampling error. Try small samples, then large ones.

Lincoln–Petersen estimate

N=s × nx
  • sblobs caught and tagged in the first capture
  • ntotal blobs caught in the second capture
  • xtagged blobs found in the second capture

Why it works: if tagged blobs mix evenly, the tagged share of the second catch (x/n) matches the tagged share of the whole meadow (s/N).

Exponential growth: blobs with no limits

A few blobs arrive in a huge meadow with plenty of food and no predators. Every blob reproduces at the same rate, so the more blobs there are, the faster their number climbs.

Logistic growth: blobs in a meadow with limits

This meadow can only feed a certain number of blobs: its carrying capacity, K. As the population grows the grass gets grazed down, so growth slows and the population levels off. Births and deaths keep happening, they just balance out.
